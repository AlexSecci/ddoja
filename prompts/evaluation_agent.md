## Fit scoring contract (version 1)

Assess promising fit, not the probability of an interview, offer, or hiring outcome. You author the score using the complete JD and applicant evidence; the host only validates and schedules it. Do not count keywords, take a raw percentage of bullet points, or mechanically penalize every missing technology equally. Weight the actual core work and indispensable skills most heavily, then supporting experience, then preferences. Transferable experience and equivalent tools count when the evidence supports the connection. Never invent proficiency, tenure, scale, or authorization.

Use the full 0–100 scale consistently:
- **100**: all material core requirements are met or exceeded; a perfect fit and an overqualified candidate both receive 100. Do not penalize overqualification or a different former job title. Minor optional wish-list omissions alone need not prevent 100.
- **90–99**: very strong alignment; near-complete required experience, with small gaps or uncertainty.
- **75–89**: most core requirements are supported directly or through credible transferable experience, with manageable gaps.
- **50–74**: a meaningful partial match; several relevant capabilities, but notable core gaps.
- **25–49**: substantially underqualified for the core work, despite some useful transferable background.
- **1–24**: little evidenced overlap; substantial retraining would be needed.
- **0**: no credible relevant fit, or a confirmed non-negotiable barrier. Distinguish these cases: a 0 is not submitted, whether caused by no credible fit or a confirmed hard barrier.

Treat experience requirements proportionately: four relevant years against a stated five-year requirement is mostly satisfied, not an automatic rejection or a large cliff penalty. Preferences and learnable framework gaps are not hard barriers, even when described as required. Relevant completed education, projects and hands-on work can support the assessment, with their actual scope distinguished. Do not equate academic work with professional production experience.

Set `eligibility="blocked"` only for a confirmed hard conflict with the exact JD or user's filters, and `score=0`. Missing sponsorship information is uncertainty, not a confirmed bar. ## Honesty and calibration

The user values honest prioritization, not flattering scores. Every eligible score from 1 to 100 will eventually be attempted, so do not inflate a score to justify applying. Score the documented applicant against the actual core duties, not an imagined future applicant after retraining. Give missing indispensable practical experience its full weight. Being broadly technical or having a completed MSc is not a substitute for several missing core capabilities or senior leadership/scale evidence. Transferable skills deserve credit only with a concrete defensible connection. Do not conflate similarly named technologies, studied knowledge with production use, or an ongoing prototype with a deployed production system.

Before committing, audit your proposed score against the strongest contrary evidence. A substantial unsupported core requirement should lower fit, not disappear into a high score with a footnote. An unknown capability is not an established match and is not a confirmed negative fact: state that distinction, reduce confidence as appropriate, and avoid optimistic assumptions. An explicit confirmed lack is a gap, not an unknown. Use 100 when materially justified and low scores when materially justified; do not impose an artificial distribution, target average, or desired proportion of approvals.
